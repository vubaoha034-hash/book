## 2. Second-pass evidence — 《凡人修仙传》

### FR2-01 — Chapter 50: competence changes relationship geometry

The scene is not retained merely by information about an opponent. A weaker-looking protagonist has prepared leverage in advance, forcing an experienced opponent to reassess him and switch from direct domination toward negotiation.

**Reader engine:** competence reveal -> opponent model changes -> new bargaining state.

**Transfer lesson:** a reveal should alter what another character can safely do now, not just tell the reader what happened earlier.

### FR2-02 — Chapter 300: conflict becomes a relationship choice

A coercive problem becomes more interesting because one participant's private loyalty threatens the whole group's survival. The pressure is not only external danger; it creates disagreement over whose life can be risked for whom.

**Reader engine:** external constraint -> loyalty conflict -> costly voluntary choice.

**Transfer lesson:** relational cost can carry page-turn pressure better than another anonymous attack.

### FR2-03 — Chapter 550: hunter and prey both have legible constraints

Pursuit is sustained because both sides have specific limitations, resources and reasons they cannot simply end the chase. The danger has ownership and causal logic.

**Reader engine:** asymmetric capabilities + reciprocal constraints -> tactical uncertainty.

**Transfer lesson:** pursuit works when the reader can reason about both sides, not when danger behaves like weather.

### FR2-04 — Chapter 800: anticipation grows during preparation, not only combat

A major dangerous zone is made interesting before entry through preparation, social gathering, alliance behavior, resource preparation and uncertainty about who will cooperate with whom.

**Reader engine:** known future event + preparation choices + social configuration.

**Transfer lesson:** a scene can create forward pull by changing readiness and relationships before the event begins.

### FR2-05 — Chapter 1051: negotiation is action

The protagonist does not merely ask for facts. He sets terms, preserves leverage, withholds trust and changes the other side's options.

**Reader engine:** bargaining changes future action permissions.

**Transfer lesson:** dialogue is strongest when each turn changes available choices, not when it forms a clean information ladder.

### FR2-06 — Chapter 1300: third forces have agendas

Multiple parties enter the same contested space with different incentives. Their appearance changes alliance possibilities and the meaning of the current conflict.

**Reader engine:** new agent -> coalition geometry changes -> reader recomputes likely outcomes.

**Transfer lesson:** a third force should not merely interrupt; it should force a new model of who may cooperate, betray or wait.

### FR2-07 — Chapter 1550: obstacle invalidates the expected solution

A known powerful method fails against an opponent with an unexpected but causally presented capability, forcing tactical adaptation.

**Reader engine:** expected competence -> failure -> immediate new problem.

**Transfer lesson:** progression retains interest when a new obstacle changes task geometry rather than only increasing numerical power.

### FR2-08 — Chapter 1800: resource value is character-specific

A large marketplace offers many impressive objects, but the protagonist filters them through his own system, needs and asymmetries. The reader follows his evaluation, not a catalog of generic rewards.

**Reader engine:** opportunity field -> selective valuation -> anticipation of what matters to this character.

**Transfer lesson:** world detail becomes propulsion when it connects to a known personal capability or future plan.

### FR2-09 — Chapter 2095: intervention reclassifies a fight

A new high-agency actor appears during an apparently legible confrontation and changes the hierarchy, motives and survival calculation.

**Reader engine:** conflict model A -> agentic intervention -> conflict model B.

**Transfer lesson:** surprise is more durable when it changes the relationship/power model, not when it is simply another projectile or pursuer.

### FR2-10 — Chapter 2312: cooperation under staged constraints

A technical task remains readable because roles, timing and failure conditions are visible. Cooperation itself becomes a source of uncertainty and trust.

**Reader engine:** shared difficult task -> role dependence -> timing risk.

**Transfer lesson:** even non-mystery scenes can pull forward when the reader understands what must go right next.

### FR2-11 — Chapter 2420: a known limit is broken

Threat escalates by violating an already meaningful expectation about what an opponent should be able to do.

**Reader engine:** known rule/limit -> credible breach -> safety model collapses.

**Transfer lesson:** escalation is strongest when it reclassifies an existing expectation rather than announcing that the enemy is simply stronger.

## 5. Cross-book mechanism convergence

### C2-01 — Present-tense choice ownership

Across the sampled books, strong scenes usually give at least one character a decision whose consequences are visible now. The reader follows what the character will do, not only what the reader will learn.

### C2-02 — Answers must alter the action model

Useful reveals change an alliance, resource, threat classification, role, plan, destination, trust relation or available option.

`answer -> new action geometry` is stronger than `answer -> more complete past explanation`.

### C2-03 — Agentic third force beats anonymous pressure

A new actor is valuable when that actor wants something and changes coalition or safety calculations. Another arrow, pursuer or interruption is weak if it has no intelligible agenda.

### C2-04 — Competence is a retention reward

Readers can continue because they trust or enjoy how a protagonist thinks under constraint. Competence must remain defeasible; a new obstacle should sometimes invalidate the expected tool.

### C2-05 — Relationship cost converts plot into consequence

Loyalty, debt, shame, affection and betrayal become propulsion when they alter present choices. Emotional prose alone is insufficient.

### C2-06 — Incidental life creates value worth threatening

Food, work, money, jokes, habits, embarrassment, petty preferences and shared history can be structurally useful precisely because they are not all clues.

### C2-07 — Reward channels should rotate

Possible continuation rewards include:

- competence reveal;
- relationship reversal;
- humor or embarrassment;
- resource gain;
- unexpected alliance;
- rule/limit breach;
- ordinary-life attachment;
- identity performance;
- physical survival;
- emotional return.

Repeated explanation reward produces fatigue even when each answer is coherent.

### C2-08 — Known rules create stronger escalation

A threat becomes meaningful when it breaks or exploits a rule the reader already understands. This produces model change rather than generic intensity.

## 6. YMGQ-specific reader-desire engine V1

This redesign uses the authoritative story kernel, not rejected Phase381 wording.

### Preserve

- protagonist identity: 刘还山;
- 顾青禾's promise: 刘还山 must deliver 顾承岳 across the legally decisive bridge boundary;
- 顾承岳 is not morally exonerated merely because he must be protected long enough to cross;
- the bridge boundary remains physically and legally decisive;
- the cost of other people's loyalty remains part of the central moral question.

### Replace the dominant engine

Old dominant engine:

`What really happened in the old case? -> obtain another fact -> explain the past -> continue escorting.`

New bounded engine:

`A loyal person chooses to help 刘还山 cross the bridge -> 顾承岳 reveals or recognizes that the post-crossing testimony will directly endanger that helper -> 刘还山 must decide what to conceal, reveal or change before the helper commits further -> the helper's loyalty makes the promise materially more expensive in the present.`

This is not a requirement to use a specific historical character from the rejected manuscript. The helper may be rebuilt, merged or replaced under the story-kernel permissions.

### Why this engine is story-specific

1. It uses the bridge's legal consequence rather than adding a generic chase.
2. It makes 顾承岳's information change 刘还山's immediate decision, not merely clarify history.
3. It turns loyalty to 刘还山 into a present cost, directly serving the central moral question.
4. It permits ordinary relationship behavior before the cost is exposed, increasing attachment.
5. The third actor has an agenda: help 刘还山 keep his promise, not simply attack or block him.
6. The reader's forward question becomes: **Will 刘还山 accept help from someone whose loyalty may be destroyed by the very testimony he is trying to deliver?**

### Representative pilot architecture — no long prose yet

**Beat A — ordinary relationship value**

A loyal helper reaches them with something practical rather than a speech: food, dry clothing, a route, a horse, medicine, or another concrete necessity. Their interaction should reveal history through behavior and irritation/affection, not biography.

**Beat B — helper commits**

The helper makes a concrete choice that increases personal exposure in order to get 刘还山 and 顾承岳 closer to the bridge.

**Beat C — recognition/reclassification**

顾承岳 recognizes a name, object, habit or relationship and realizes the helper is implicated by what will happen after crossing. He does not deliver a clean explanatory packet; the information should emerge under character pressure.

**Beat D — immediate decision problem**

刘还山 now has a practical choice: accept the helper's next commitment, warn them and risk losing the crossing opportunity, conceal the danger, or change the route/plan at a real cost.

**Beat E — end on action ownership, not epigram**

The pilot ends after 刘还山 makes or postpones a costly action in a way that creates a concrete next-page question. Do not close with a polished thematic summary.

## 7. Anti-AI constraints for this pilot

These remain coordinator constraints, not a phrase blacklist:

- Do not use repeated question -> exact answer -> exact follow-up ladders.
- Do not immediately explain the meaning of every gesture or emotional beat.
- Allow the helper and 刘还山 to have attention that is not optimized for the plot.
- Do not make 顾承岳 emotionally omniscient; he may misread, avoid or prioritize the wrong thing.
- Permit one or two ordinary/frictional details that are not monetized into clues.
- Do not force a dramatic one-line closure.
