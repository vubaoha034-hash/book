# PHASE385 — Cross-Book Reader Propulsion Evidence Cards V1

Status: SOURCE-DERIVED / ABSTRACT ONLY / NO PROTECTED PROSE

Purpose: record located source evidence for why readers keep reading, without copying prose or treating one book's mechanism as universal.

## 《诡秘之主》 — current uploaded file is partial (~520 chapters)

### LOTM-01 — Chapter 1 opening
- Surface: immediate bodily pain and sensory anomaly before cosmology explanation.
- Reader desire before: what happened to the protagonist?
- State change: ordinary waking becomes impossible-body-survival problem.
- Desire after: why is he alive after an apparently fatal wound, and what is this world?
- Key craft: answer arrives through physical inspection, not exposition-first worldbuilding.

### LOTM-02 — Chapters 5–7, first recurring social engine
- Surface: an experiment unexpectedly connects strangers; protagonist improvises a role instead of merely learning lore.
- Reader desire before: can the protagonist use the ritual to return home?
- State change: failed/altered experiment creates a new social identity and recurring meeting structure.
- Desire after: can he maintain the role, what can he gain, and what will the others reveal?
- Key craft: one mystery answer creates a repeatable story machine.

### LOTM-03 — Family/economy ordinary-life beats
- Surface: rent, food, work, sibling misunderstanding, small jokes and embarrassment remain visible between supernatural beats.
- Reader function: characters have lives that are not merely clue delivery.
- Key craft: ordinary life creates contrast and emotional cost; it also makes later danger feel like intrusion into something valuable.

### LOTM-04 — Chapter 78 physical re-entry hook
- Surface: apparent closure around a corpse is broken by a concrete bodily reversal.
- Reader desire change: from processing the aftermath to immediate danger/question.
- Key craft: chapter-end propulsion can be physical and specific; no narrator needs to announce its importance.

### LOTM-05 — Mid-volume social/comic chapter ending
- Surface: a family misunderstanding ends on character-specific humor rather than threat.
- Reader function: continuation desire can come from affection/amusement, not only cliffhanger.
- Key craft: varied reward/ending types prevent one cadence from becoming visible.

### LOTM-06 — Volume-level author technical notes embedded in source
- Source-derived author self-diagnosis: fixed recurrence began to feel repetitive; some low-value repetitions were skipped. Event rhythm that stayed too tight was recognized as fatiguing. Minor unresolved lines were suppressed near a major ending so they would not dilute the emotional picture.
- Key craft: human long-form control includes deletion, omission and relaxation; maximum event density is not maximum retention.

### LOTM-07 — Volume ending architecture
- Surface: local arc closes emotionally while major enemies/questions remain in the next setting.
- Reader desire after closure: grief/reclassification plus a concrete new destination and unfinished targets.
- Key craft: payoff and continuation coexist; the book does not keep every question artificially unanswered.

### LOTM-08 — Prose surface
- Stratified sample: median chapter ~3253 chars; median paragraph ~31 chars; dialogue paragraphs ~34%.
- Interpretation: short paragraphs and dialogue are common but not exclusive. The prose alternates internal thought, social detail, observation, action and explanation rather than presenting every beat as a dramatic one-line emphasis.

## 《凡人修仙传》 — narrative reaches finale; EPUB requires cleaning for final quantitative claims

### FR-01 — Chapter 1 ordinary desire
- Surface: poor rural family, credible small hopes, affection toward family, desire to see a larger world.
- Reader desire before: can an ordinary child leave his limited life?
- State change: an opportunity enters through a relative.
- Desire after: can he qualify, and what will leaving cost?
- Key craft: emotional/aspirational clarity precedes the fantasy system.

### FR-02 — Early sect test
- Surface: social class differences, physical test, uncertain admission.
- Reader function: progress itself becomes suspense.
- Key craft: the reader understands the goal and rules before the test; no mystery is required.

### FR-03 — Chapter 10 durable resource
- Surface: an apparently small found object becomes a long-running asymmetric resource.
- Reader desire change: from “can he survive training?” to “what can this resource become if used carefully?”
- Key craft: a reward opens a long chain of future options instead of closing tension.

### FR-04 — Around Chapter 60, temptation/decision
- Surface: protagonist is offered a seductive cooperation path and refuses to surrender decision control.
- Reader reward: competence, caution and independent judgment.
- Key craft: reader attachment is partly trust in how the protagonist reasons.

### FR-05 — Around Chapter 180, trap logic
- Surface: danger emerges through other actors deliberately manipulating environment/creatures rather than arbitrary author attack.
- Reader function: threat has causal ownership and adversarial intelligence.
- Key craft: readers can fear an outcome because they understand how the trap works.

### FR-06 — Mid-book recurring old connections
- Surface: previously known people/factions recur under changed power/status conditions.
- Reader reward: long memory matters; progression reclassifies old relationships.
- Key craft: scale growth does not require discarding earlier narrative assets.

### FR-07 — Progression engine
- Repeated macro-chain: bottleneck -> preparation/resource -> risk/decision -> gain -> new capability -> larger environment -> new bottleneck.
- Key craft: each reward changes the possibility space. The reader follows not just “what happens” but “what the protagonist can now do.”

### FR-08 — Prose surface
- Cleaner stratified sample: median chapter ~3019 chars; median paragraph ~41 chars; dialogue paragraphs ~22%.
- Interpretation: substantial exposition and longer practical paragraphs coexist with high retention. Therefore “explanation is AI” or “long paragraphs are bad” are invalid rules.

## 《凡人修仙之仙界篇》 — complete clean 1394 chapters

### FRI-01 — Chapter 1 threat-first opening
- Surface: vulnerable character under pursuit; mysterious powerful figure appears through a physical disruption.
- Reader desire after: who is he, what is his state, and what will he do?
- Key craft: identity mystery is bound to immediate survival, not separated into lore explanation.

### FRI-02 — Chapters 2–4 relationship anchor
- Surface: threat is resolved, then a protective relationship forms before the next expansion.
- Reader reward: safety/affection after danger.
- Key craft: emotional bond makes the powerful mysterious character worth following beyond combat.

### FRI-03 — Chapters 5–10 chained state changes
- Surface: new hazard -> new actors -> temporary shelter -> helper becomes threat -> ritual danger.
- Reader function: almost every chapter changes the current physical/relational situation.
- Key craft: continuation comes from unstable present state rather than only long-range mystery.

### FRI-04 — Mid-book escalation
- Surface: resource/realm/action problems are repeatedly translated into concrete new obstacles, not only numerical power statements.
- Key craft: progression needs changing task geometry, not just bigger numbers.

### FRI-05 — Late-book threshold scenes
- Surface: major barriers are represented as actions the protagonist must physically/strategically cross.
- Reader reward: accumulated power is demonstrated through a previously meaningful limit.
- Key craft: payoff is stronger when growth is measured against a known obstacle.

### FRI-06 — Complete ending availability
- The current source contains all 1394 chapters, enabling future whole-book arc, climax and ending-payoff analysis without extrapolation.

### FRI-07 — Prose surface
- Stratified sample: median chapter ~3192 chars; median paragraph ~36 chars; dialogue paragraphs ~24%.
- Interpretation: action-heavy prose still uses mixed paragraph lengths and substantial connective narration; it is not built from continuous punch-line beats.

## Cross-book evidence synthesis

### COMMON-01 — Future desire, not information count
All three books repeatedly change what the reader wants to see next. More facts alone are not enough.

### COMMON-02 — Agency
Main characters test, choose, prepare, bargain, hide, spend, risk or exploit. They are not primarily passive receivers of explanation.

### COMMON-03 — Reward opens the next lock
A solved problem often creates a new role, resource, enemy, bottleneck, obligation or larger model.

### COMMON-04 — Varied continuation mechanisms
Threat, humor, affection, resource gain, identity performance, social embarrassment, model reclassification and promised confrontation can all carry continuation desire.

### COMMON-05 — Incidental life
Not every detail is immediately converted into plot information. Ordinary concerns make characters/worlds feel inhabited and provide contrast.

### COMMON-06 — Causal trust
The reader can usually trace why the character takes the next action from known information, incentives, resource constraints and risk.

### COMMON-07 — Anti-template lesson for YMGQ
The current YMGQ problem is not merely short dialogue. It is over-regularized author control: clean question/answer ladders, immediate semantic closure, repeated emphasis paragraphing, and characters whose attention is optimized for plot facts.

## Current transfer hypothesis for YMGQ

The next YMGQ pilot should test a different engine:
- establish a concrete future-facing decision/expectation owned by the character;
- let one answer materially alter that future decision rather than simply clarify the past;
- allow some social/ordinary material to exist without clue function;
- reduce immediate narrator explanation of what each emotional beat means;
- vary scene closure rather than always ending on a designed dramatic sentence.

This remains a labelled-manuscript hypothesis until actual human reading supports it.
