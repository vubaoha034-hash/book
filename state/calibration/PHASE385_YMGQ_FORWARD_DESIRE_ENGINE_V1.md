## 2. Old propulsion engine to retire

`retrospective clue -> explanation -> another old fact -> generic pursuit -> move prisoner closer to bridge`

Why it fails for the labelled target reader:

- the next physical action is predictable;
- many rewards are historical explanations;
- 顾承岳 behaves too much like an information container;
- danger does not sufficiently change the moral decision;
- emotional prose can improve while desire-to-continue stays low.

## 3. New engine hypothesis

### Primary future-facing question

**If crossing the bridge fulfills 刘还山's promise but also turns 顾承岳's evidence into an immediate legal cost for 刘还山 himself, will Liu still take the final step?**

Pilot-only mechanism: before the crossing, Liu discovers that a sealed statement carried by Gu names Liu as a material participant/witness in the case. Once Gu enters imperial custody, that statement becomes actionable. This is not yet permanent canon; it is a bounded propulsion hypothesis.

### Secondary relationship question

**If someone loyal to 刘还山 chooses to cross with him and share that legal/personal cost, will Liu accept that loyalty or force the person to remain free on the west side?**

This converts loyalty from sentimental backstory into a present decision.

### Gu Chengyue's agency

顾承岳 wants to cross, but not because he is simply seeking rescue. He has his own reason to submit the statement and accepts that it may harm Liu. He is therefore capable of helping the crossing succeed while also making Liu's promise harder to keep.

This prevents the prisoner from functioning only as protected cargo or a confession machine.

## 4. Scene-state machine

### State A — A concrete possible tomorrow
Before maximum pressure, show a mundane plan that could happen after the crossing: food, sleep, paying a debt, returning an item, sending someone home. It must belong to characters, not function as symbolic decoration.

Reader desire: “Can they actually reach that small tomorrow?”

### State B — The cost is discovered
A physical trace/document/action reveals that crossing will create a new consequence for Liu. Do not explain every implication immediately.

Reader desire changes from “can he get Gu across?” to “will he still choose to?”

### State C — Loyalty enters the price
A companion independently chooses to follow Liu across despite the consequence.

Liu now has three meaningful options:
1. keep promise and accept the companion's sacrifice;
2. keep promise but sever/refuse the companion's loyalty;
3. break/alter the promise to prevent the cost.

No option is clean.

### State D — Gu acts
Gu makes a choice that protects his own objective but does not conveniently resolve Liu's dilemma. His action should reprice the crossing rather than deliver a complete explanation.

### State E — Bridge threshold
The climax-adjacent pressure is the final physical step into the legal boundary. Spectacle can exist, but the peak must be the narrowing moral choice, not the number of attackers.

## 5. Reward palette

Do not repeat only “new fact” rewards. Rotate:

- ordinary companionship / humor;
- character competence;
- relationship permission or refusal;
- concrete gain with a cost;
- model reclassification;
- a decision becoming irreversible;
- brief safety or imaginable future;
- moral consequence.

## 6. Anti-AI surface controls for this pilot

These are manuscript-specific controls, not universal bans:

- Do not narrate the meaning immediately after every readable gesture.
- Do not make every paragraph end with a polished contrast/reversal.
- Let characters misread, avoid, interrupt and focus on practical things.
- Allow some ordinary detail to remain ordinary.
- High-information dialogue must arise from what the speaker needs now.
- Do not use a reusable `question -> exact answer -> correct inference -> decision` ladder.
- Vary closure: some segments may end on action, unfinished speech, practical inconvenience or changed behavior rather than an epigram.
- No phrase blacklist.
