# Dramatization Surface Density V1

## Purpose

Catch manuscripts whose major scenes are logically correct and locally grounded but repeatedly collapse into event-skeleton presentation: terse functional dialogue, thin blocking and summary-like transitions with too little lived pressure, perception, relational friction or consequential behavior.

This is a manuscript-level family check. It must not be reduced to whether one isolated short exchange is valid.

## Required cross-scene record

For material scenes, record:

- scene function;
- emotional / relational pressure entering;
- dominant surface carrier: dialogue / action / perception / environment / interiority / object / silence;
- whether the carrier changes the reader's felt state or only transmits event state;
- whether omitted emotion is legible from specific shared history or only assumed by the author;
- whether characters resist, misread, defer, interrupt, conceal or physically negotiate the pressure;
- whether the scene has an afterimage / aftershock beyond a transition to the next plot beat;
- portability: would the same sparse dialogue mechanism work with unrelated characters after swapping names?

## Failure codes

`SCENE_SURFACE_SKELETON_DENSITY_RISK`
: separated high-stakes scenes across different relationships repeatedly use the same stripped functional surface, so the manuscript reads like an efficient event outline even when local logic is correct.

`DRAMATIC_PRESSURE_NOT_CONVERTED_TO_LIVED_SCENE`
: a scene has substantial underlying stakes but the prose does not convert them into specific resistance, perception, behavior, subtext or consequence that the reader can experience.

## Important distinction

`SHORT_DIALOGUE != SKELETON`.

A short exchange may be excellent when a unique object, physical constraint, silence, shared history or irreversible action makes the emotional information unavoidable.

The failure requires recurrence or a major scene whose available dramatic pressure is materially under-realized.

## False-positive controls

Do not require:

- longer dialogue;
- decorative sensory description;
- generic pauses, glances or clenched fists;
- lyrical prose;
- interior monologue in every scene.

The goal is not padding. The goal is conversion of underlying dramatic pressure into a felt scene.
