# Suspense Curiosity Charge V1.1

## Purpose

V1 separates working hypothesis from curiosity charge. V1.1 separates **danger interest** from **answer interest** so an evaluator cannot infer that a mystery is compelling merely because the events around it are dangerous.

## 1. Answer-value ablation test

For each carried mystery, ask:

`If the correct answer to the mystery were revealed now, while all current danger/action remained, how much reader pull would disappear?`

If most pull remains because survival, combat, friendship or immediate danger carries the scene, the mystery itself may have weak curiosity charge.

Do not literally rewrite the story; this is an audit counterfactual.

## 2. Question-specific prediction test

State a prediction that depends on the mystery answer, not merely on the fact that danger exists.

Bad:
`someone may die because the hidden force is dangerous.`

Better:
`if this hidden force is actually connected to X, then character Y's loyalty/choice/identity will be reclassified and the next action must change.`

If no answer-specific prediction exists, record low charge risk.

## 3. Mystery ownership test

Distinguish:

- threat ownership: protagonist must survive/respond;
- answer ownership: protagonist personally needs to know what the answer means for a valued relationship, guilt, identity, choice, plan or belief.

Threat ownership alone does not prove answer ownership.

## 4. Clue-desire delta test

For each clue, record two separate changes:

- model delta: what theory changes?
- desire delta: why do I now want the answer more?

A chain can pass model progression while failing desire progression.

## 5. Payoff appetite test

Before the reveal, ask what concrete emotional/strategic reclassification the reader is waiting to experience when the answer arrives.

If the answer mainly supplies attribution (`who was behind this`) after the dangerous function is already understood, payoff appetite may be low unless identity has independent consequence.

## Failure codes

Inherited V1 codes remain active.

`DANGER_CARRIES_PULL_BUT_MYSTERY_ANSWER_DOES_NOT`
: scenes are compelling because of immediate threat/action, but revealing the mystery answer early would remove little reader pull.

`MYSTERY_HAS_MODEL_DELTA_WITHOUT_DESIRE_DELTA`
: clues refine the theory without increasing appetite for the answer.

`ANSWER_IDENTITY_HAS_WEAK_RECLASSIFICATION_VALUE`
: the reveal mainly labels the hidden actor/object without substantially changing a cared-about relationship, choice, belief or operational plan.

## False-positive controls

- A tactical mystery may pass if answer identity directly changes survival strategy.
- A pure puzzle may pass when evidence play itself is the promised reader reward.
- Do not demand melodrama or personal betrayal in every mystery.
- Do not confuse complete ending with weak suspense.

## Core distinction

`I care whether they survive != I care what the mystery answer is`.
