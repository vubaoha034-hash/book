# Dramatization Surface Density V1.1

## Purpose

Prevent unique names, props or backstory from automatically rescuing a manuscript whose separated high-stakes scenes repeatedly use the same thin event-transmission surface.

V1 already checks surface recurrence. V1.1 adds a **functional-mask test** and an **unused-pressure test**.

## 1. Functional-mask test

For every material emotional scene, temporarily abstract away:

- character names;
- unique object names;
- faction names;
- lore labels;
- plot-specific nouns.

Record only the surface sequence, for example:

`question -> short answer -> instruction -> short resistance -> exit`

or

`arrival -> explanation packet -> brief reaction -> next plot action`.

Then compare separated scenes.

If many unrelated scenes become substantially interchangeable after masking their nouns, unique props/history are functioning as labels on the same shallow scene machine rather than as active dramatization.

## 2. Active-carrier test

A unique object/history detail only counts as dramatization if it changes at least one of:

- what a character can physically do;
- what they refuse to do;
- what they misread;
- what they interrupt or leave unsaid;
- how power/relationship changes during the scene;
- what irreversible action follows;
- what the reader newly experiences rather than merely understands.

Mere mention of chicken, wine, shield, arrows, old debt or travel history does not by itself differentiate scene surface.

## 3. Unused-pressure test

List the strongest scene-specific pressures already available before the scene.

Ask whether the surface actually lets those pressures alter behavior, perception, timing, speech order or consequence.

If several high-value pressures exist but the scene still resolves mainly through terse functional turns, record under-realization.

## 4. Manuscript-level accumulation test

Do not judge from one short exchange.

Search for separated high-stakes scenes across different relationships that repeatedly reduce to the same masked sequence with little active-carrier conversion.

A manuscript may contain excellent minimal scenes and still fail if minimal event-skeleton presentation becomes the dominant surface habit.

## Failure codes

`SCENE_SURFACE_SKELETON_DENSITY_RISK`
: inherited from V1; now requires functional-mask evidence across separated scenes.

`UNIQUE_PROPS_LABEL_SAME_THIN_SCENE_MACHINE`
: surface appears varied because props/history differ, but after masking nouns the same shallow transition remains and the props do not materially alter scene behavior.

`AVAILABLE_DRAMATIC_PRESSURE_LEFT_UNUSED`
: a high-stakes scene has multiple specific pressures available, but most do not enter behavior, perception, timing, speech order or consequence before the scene advances.

`LOCALLY_LIVED_SCENES_DO_NOT_CANCEL_MANUSCRIPT_SURFACE_FLATNESS`
: several strong scenes are present, yet the manuscript-level dominant surface across many other high-stakes scenes remains event-skeletonized.

## False-positive controls

- A single terse task exchange does not fail.
- A short scene may pass when one active carrier makes the conflict unavoidable.
- Do not demand decorative description, interior monologue, longer dialogue or generic gestures.
- Different scenes need not use different techniques merely for variety; the issue is repeated under-conversion of available pressure.
