# Suspense Curiosity Charge V1

## Purpose

Distinguish `the reader can form a hypothesis` from `the reader strongly wants to resolve the mystery`.

A technically fair mystery may still be inert.

## Required record for a carried mystery

In addition to the V4 hypothesis chain, record:

- why this question matters to a person/relationship/choice the reader already cares about;
- what prediction the current hypothesis creates;
- what the reader fears/hopes will happen if the hypothesis is true;
- what evidence would produce a meaningful emotional or strategic reclassification;
- whether each clue changes desire-to-resolve, not only information state;
- whether the protagonist has personal ownership of the question or merely tactical awareness;
- payoff desirability: before the answer, is there a concrete reason to want the answer now?

## Failure codes

`SUSPENSE_HAS_WORKING_MODEL_BUT_LOW_CURIOSITY_CHARGE`
: the reader can state a plausible model but has weak desire to resolve it because the answer is abstract, generic, low-stakes or insufficiently tied to a cared-about relationship/choice.

`SUSPENSE_CLUES_UPDATE_MODEL_BUT_NOT_READER_DESIRE`
: clue progression changes the theory while subjective interest remains flat.

`MYSTERY_LACKS_PERSONAL_OR_DECISION_OWNERSHIP`
: the mystery remains an external puzzle and does not materially constrain a protagonist choice, relationship or feared consequence.

## False-positive controls

Not every mystery must be emotional. A pure puzzle can pass when solving it has strong operational stakes, distinctive evidence play and genuine prediction tension.

Do not require identity mystery, melodrama or a twist. Require meaningful desire to resolve.

## Core distinction

`WORKING_HYPOTHESIS != CURIOSITY_CHARGE`.
